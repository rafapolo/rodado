Financial Markets, Investment Funds & Capital Structure

Five banks hold 59% of the financial system's assets and 65% of its credit. Borrowers earning up to one minimum wage are behind on 9.3% of what they owe, and overdraft users on 15.7%.

Brazil's Central Bank publishes three portraits of the financial system. IF.data carries the balance sheet of each of some 1,400 conglomerates and institutions. SCR.data shows the country's credit portfolio by product, borrower income and arrears. The Pix statistics count, municipality by municipality, how much people and companies transfer. Together they show a concentrated banking market, household credit that weighs more on those who earn less, and a public payments infrastructure that changed daily life in five years.

Banking concentration: five conglomerates, 59% of assets

In June 2026 the financial system held R$ 18.9 trillion in assets. Itaú, Banco do Brasil, Caixa, Bradesco and Santander held 58.9% of that total, 64.7% of the credit portfolio and 63.6% of funding. Two of the five are state-owned. BNDES ranks sixth, and the largest digital bank, Nubank, has 1.9% of assets.

Household credit: payroll loans 3.7% in arrears, overdrafts 15.7%

In July 2026 the credit portfolio totaled R$ 7.58 trillion, R$ 4.67 trillion to individuals and R$ 2.91 trillion to companies. Household non-performing loans (over 90 days late) were 5.8%, nearly twice the corporate rate (3.0%). The gap between products is far larger. Payroll-deducted loans run at 3.7% in arrears; personal loans without payroll deduction at 11.3%; overdrafts at 15.7%; revolving credit card debt at 14.1%. Mortgages, backed by the property, sit at 1.3%.

Income and arrears: lower earners fall further behind

The same register splits credit by borrower income. Among those earning up to one minimum wage, 9.3% of the debt is more than 90 days late; between 1 and 2 minimum wages, 7.7%. From 5 minimum wages up, arrears stay between 4% and 6%. Among borrowers recorded as having no income, they reach 20.3%. The bracket above 20 minimum wages has the largest portfolio, R$ 986 billion, more than the two brackets up to 2 minimum wages combined (R$ 910 billion).

Pix: from R$ 4.5 trillion to R$ 29.5 trillion in four years

Created by the Central Bank at the end of 2020, Pix moved R$ 29.5 trillion in 71.1 billion transactions in 2025, counting only transactions with an identified municipality. In 2021, its first full year, it moved R$ 4.5 trillion. Individuals made 62.0 billion payments, averaging R$ 189. Companies made 9.1 billion, averaging R$ 1,960, and accounted for 60% of the value.

Pix by region: the North pays most often per resident

Divided by 2022 Census population, individuals' Pix payments in 2025 come to 379 transactions per resident in the North, 322 in the Center-West, 319 in the Northeast, 298 in the Southeast and 248 in the South. In regions with fewer bank branches and less card use, Pix has become the everyday means of payment.

Explanatory hypotheses

Brazil's banking concentration results from decades of mergers and the privatization of state banks, alongside large public banks that never competed with private ones on price. With little competition, spreads stay high and the most expensive credit reaches those with the least collateral. The gap in arrears between payroll loans and unsecured credit shows that risk is charged to those with no payroll to offer. Pix is the counterexample: a public infrastructure, open to any institution, slashed the cost of moving money and brought in competitors that the credit market, on its own, did not.

Policy implications

The Pix model, an open public infrastructure, can be extended to credit: Open Finance and easier portability let customers take their payment history to another institution and get lower rates. Caps on overdraft and revolving card costs, already in place, need monitoring so borrowers are not pushed into even more expensive products. Renegotiation programs such as Desenrola relieve the stock of debt but do not change the flow; that requires financial education and secured credit accessible to lower income brackets.
